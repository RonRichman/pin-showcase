The effect of a driver’s age can depend on their claims history. The effect of a car’s power can depend on its age.

These are interactions: a feature’s effect changes with another feature. PIN learns a collection of two-feature functions, then combines them into a prediction.

For the curious What makes this “tree-like”? +

The centered hard sigmoid is σ(z) = max(0, min(1, (1 + z) / 2)). It is exactly 0 below −1, exactly 1 above 1, and linear between them. Applied after a nonlinear network, it produces regions separated by learned boundaries. PIN is a neural network trained by gradient methods; it does not grow a conventional decision tree.

In the illustration, z = 3(cos(θ)x + sin(θ)y − t). The direction θ and offset t are your controls. The published architecture learns a more flexible function of the two embedded inputs.

02 / INSIDE PIN

A shared engine.
A distinct token for every pair.

One network.
Many relationships.

All pairs use the same interaction network. Each pair also has its own learned token.

This is how PIN shares its modelling capacity while allowing different relationships to behave differently. Follow the path from a policy’s features to its predicted claim frequency.

Technical view The prediction, written out +

log λ(x) = b + ∑j ≤ k wjk hjk(xj, xk)

Each hjk is the hard sigmoid of the shared network applied to two feature embeddings and a learned pair token. Terms with j = k depend on one feature; terms with j < k depend on two. The exponential gives claim frequency λ(x), and exposure × λ(x) gives the expected claim count.

The additive structure is on the log-frequency scale. Applying the exponential changes the scale. Contributions are not additive in claim counts or premiums, and individual pair units are not a unique statistical separation of main effects and pure interactions.

The paper specifies a French embedding dimension of 10 and shared hidden layers of 30 and 20 units. The repository’s saved-model implementation has a continuous-embedding activation placement difference, documented with the model exhibits below.

Why pairwise matters An efficient route to Shapley values +

On the link scale, PIN contains effects involving at most two features. For the paper’s interventional value function, averaging a feature ordering and its reverse gives the exact Shapley allocation for a fixed background distribution. Sampling the background still approximates the population expectation.

The exhibits use a single saved model and a fixed finite background. The exact pairwise shortcut does not automatically apply to the log of an ensemble formed by averaging predicted frequencies.

A CLEAR VIEW OF THE CLAIM

What the evidence
does—and doesn’t—say.

Two datasets. A promising result.

The paper supports predictive performance and inspectable pairwise structure on the French and Belgian motor frequency tasks. Rankings refer to the reported comparators and evaluation protocols.

Pairs are a deliberate restriction.

The log predictor models effects involving up to two features. It cannot freely represent arbitrary higher-order effects. This restriction is also what makes its components and Shapley explanations tractable.

Explanation needs a reference.

Feature dependence, the chosen background and data support affect interpretation. A learned relationship is an association in a model, not evidence of causality.
